## Proposed exploration for Opus to scope

Keep current r2 as the comparison. First extend the response to the existing forward catch, seated fall and knees-first collapse in T2.5, checking each contact sequence. Then preview a small variety set before implementing the entire Stage 3 outline:

- **Sideways spill:** hip/side contact, torso follows, one arm reacts and the body settles onto back or front. Adds a new silhouette and lateral momentum; suitable for an off-balance shove or oblique strike. Needs an authored transition, not merely a rotated back bump.
- **Seated collapse:** hips drop first, shoulders rock back, legs kick and settle. Builds on sitBack; useful for exhaustion or a stunned knockdown. Avoid the same full-body hop as a flat-back slam.
- **Forward catch and buckle:** hands/forearms yield, knees or chest follow, heels kick. Builds on forward/kneesForward; useful when balance fails forward. Distinguish supported compression from unsupported flight.
- **Heavy flat-back bump:** strongest contrast against the existing slam; shoulders/hips compress, legs recoil at different times, one dominant rebound, smaller settling motion, then a longer stunned beat.

Select by attack direction, move intent, balance and ring room; randomize only within compatible choices. Fatigue can bias collapse versus recovery effort. Do not add a stumble before a throw whose clip already supplies the flight.

## Bigger impact comparison

For one slam, show **current / heavy / exceptional** side by side at normal and quarter speed, same rigs, facing, incoming motion and deterministic sell choice. Explore the heavy version first. Starting candidates, not final specifications: current 8.8 px commanded hop; heavy about 11–12 px; exceptional about 14–16 px, only if the pose and timing support it. The current 14 px cap means the exceptional candidate would require an explicit tuning change, not simply higher intensity.

Shape the impact as well as its height: stronger short compression, greater but staggered knee/arm recoil, a dominant first rebound, a much smaller settling reaction, and a brief spent-body pause before pain/recovery. Avoid stretching the pre-impact hang time just to make the hit bigger. Align any stronger mat sound and brief camera impulse to the same single impact. Presentation intensity must not silently change damage, pin eligibility or down time; review those gameplay effects separately, since landing duration currently gates pins/AI pounces.

Reserve the exceptional response for a small set of major moves or match moments so ordinary bumps retain contrast. Larger hops must not turn every fall into repeated springing. Preserve head/neck intent, signed joint limits and contact continuity; correct problematic keys rather than enlarging joint ranges. Keep this authored and bounded within the existing pipeline.
