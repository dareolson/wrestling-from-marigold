## Proposed visual target

A worn illustrated wrestling weekly: blunt ink shapes, limited muted color, rough brush edges, and economical compositions. Private scenes feel like working rooms inhabited by tired people. Public scenes can be theatrical while sharing the same drawing rules.

- Faces: two or three main value shapes, selective lines, no pores or individually rendered stubble. Preserve Carl's proportions, moustache, hairline and warmth.
- Materials: towel reads through its silhouette, folds and a few dry marks; no fiber rendering. Simplify cloth, brick and skin to the same detail scale.
- Palette: soot, dirty cream, tobacco, faded workwear blue and restrained oxblood. Keep skin hues distinct. Practical lamps create localized light; avoid bathing every scene in gold.
- Edges: irregular ink and dry paint, deliberate lost edges in shadow, less uniformly crisp detail. Reserve contrast for faces, hands and significant props.
- Finish: subtle fixed paper texture and restrained color breakup after the drawing works. Preserve dialogue clarity. Dense halftone and regular hatching remain excluded by the existing standard unless Derek changes that rule.
- Resolution experiment: compare a composition authored for 480×300 and displayed at 960×600 against a simplified 960×600 drawing. Choose by faces and atmosphere at runtime size. Downsampling a detailed painting alone will not establish the style.

Small next art trial: use the existing Carl/Guy wrist-taping two-shot as the controlled composition. Produce one gritty ink-and-flat-color treatment; if useful, a second rough gouache treatment. ## Story spine

Guy begins wanting a chance to prove he can fight. He must learn the craft, earn trust, recognize exploitation, and eventually gain enough standing to choose what kind of professional he will be. Triumph means a livelihood and a place he has earned, as well as the final victory. Retain ambition and joy: hardship has weight when there is something worth reaching for.

Show recurring costs through a few changing details: the purse, missed yard hours, taped injuries, borrowed robe, transport home, and whether another wrestler will sit beside him. Avoid making every scene a new disaster. Let competence, humor and friendship accumulate too.

Carl has stakes of his own. Putting Guy on the card costs him favors and credibility. He can advocate patience when Guy needs to resist, and Guy can mistake useful criticism for lack of faith. Their disagreement should change through observed actions rather than a speech in every scene. The existing borrowed robe and promised MORETTI robe are strong recurring objects: first belonging, then responsibility, finally something Guy can offer another newcomer.

## Every opponent needs a relationship

For each feud, write five concrete beats: an interaction before the first bell, a personal stake on both sides, an incident in the match, a response afterward, and a changed rematch. Give the opponent something to lose besides ranking. At least one later scene should remember the relationship. Some opponents become colleagues or friends; personal does not always mean hostile.

Possible roles for the existing seven-opponent structure, pending roster assignment:

| Role | Interaction and professional conflict | Match consequence / resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House Favorite | Treats Guy as disposable help; notices the crowd reacting and fears losing his place | Opening losses become scouting; the upset changes how he addresses Guy, even if he refuses friendship |
| Featured Heel | Initially offers useful advice, then expects obedience and attacks Guy's growing independence | Manipulates pace and provokes mistakes; rematch tests restraint and adaptation |
| Working veteran | Shares practical help, then admits Guy's opportunity may cost him a booking | Honest technical contest; respect can survive either result |
| Office enforcer | Publicly presents himself as a professional doing a job; privately makes the consequences explicit | Tests whether Guy can defend himself without becoming reckless or cruel |
| Fellow prospect | Offers companionship and shares Guy's ambitions; only one gets the next opportunity | Competitive friendship fractures or matures through the result |
| Established contender | Gives Guy credit but doubts his endurance and reliability | Longer contest tests accumulated craft; later acknowledges the work rather than granting instant affection |
| Champion | Regards Guy as talented but unproven over a season; embodies the position Guy hopes to hold | Final series draws on earlier lessons; winning carries responsibility for the next man coming up |

These are dramatic functions, not new roster commitments or claims about historical wrestlers. Assign actual characters only after deciding the fiction/history boundaries.

One structural question to revisit separately: the opening currently combines professional performance objectives with strong artificial disadvantages and chapter-reset losses.
