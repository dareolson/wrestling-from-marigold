# Carnival opening — Guido earns his way in

2026-10-31 · Codex recording Derek's revised opening direction. User-specified premise below supersedes the bricklayer origin and Marigold arrival as the first playable story event. Dialogue and implementation choices are drafts, distinguished below. No campaign runtime or rig has been implemented by this document.

## Derek's direction

Guido was a steelworker, now out of paid work because he is on strike. Rent is one week overdue. His landlord demands $100 by tomorrow and threatens eviction. His girlfriend demands that he return to work despite the picket line, then sends him out to find the money. This latest detailed scene supersedes the earlier landlord suggestion to cross the line. Guido goes for a walk, finds a carnival advertising $100 to anyone who beats its wrestling champion, enters, and wins. **This carnival bout is the first playable match.** Carl sees him, offers an opportunity, meets Guido's refusal, and answers that he has money.

Use the 1952 steel strike as the historical peg; the landlord, carnival, prize contest, champion and recruitment are campaign fiction. The June–July strike window fits the summer setting. A striking worker need not have lost his job permanently: describe him as on strike/out of work, not as already fired unless Derek adds that fact.

Historical anchors: [Chicago-area steelworkers, Encyclopedia of Chicago](https://encyclopedia.chicagohistory.org/pages/653.html); [Truman's June 10, 1952 statement describing the June 2 strike](https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/public-papers/161/special-message-congress-steel-strike). Do not claim this fictional carnival actually took place.

## Draft playable sequence

1. **Skyline and strike montage.** First shot: Chicago skyline with the title/headline **CHICAGO 1952**. A radio announcer supplies the strike backstory. Cut to picket signs and obscured people; these inserts may be atmospheric, abstract or documentary in feeling rather than roster-like cartoon portraits. Exact radio copy and sign wording remain to be written.
2. **Kitchen phone call.** Guido calls his union boss on a telephone attached to the kitchen wall. No deal; the strike is expected to continue another month. Guido hangs up, dismayed. This is the character's outlook, not a documentary statement about the strike's actual remaining duration.
3. **Landlord, then girlfriend.** A knock interrupts him. The landlord demands the overdue $100 by tomorrow and threatens eviction. Guido slams the door. His girlfriend emerges and tells him to go back to work. Guido refuses to cross the line because he would be ostracized. She dismisses the other workers and orders him out to find $100. See the authored scene below.
4. **Walk.** Guido leaves under the deadline to find money. Sounds of a cheap midway interrupt his aimless search: barker, bell, a few onlookers laughing. Keep this a short transition, not a new open-world walking system.
5. **The challenge.** Editable sign/barker copy: **$100 TO THE MAN WHO BEATS THE CHAMPION.** Show an impressive but cheesy professional strongman. Guido signs up as Guido Moretti. The money, not a childhood dream of fame, gets him through the ropes.
6. **Player takes control.** First actual match, using shared wrestling mechanics. Introduce movement, striking, grapple and pin in short contextual prompts. The champion is beatable through clear openings; the player earns the win. Avoid a cinematic auto-win or a heavily scripted seven-minute finish here.
7. **Payout.** Show Guido actually receiving the promised $100. This is the payoff for the immediate rent pressure. Prize withheld / promoter swindle is not part of Derek's pitch and should not be added by default.
8. **Carl.** Carl has watched from the crowd. He approaches after the result and offers paid wrestling work. Guido initially refuses: he entered for rent, not a career. Carl's money offer changes the conversation.
9. **Bridge to Marigold.** A later appointment/booking leads into the existing exterior, trainer conversation and professional wrestling arc. The old arrival/taping art can serve this later debut rather than being discarded.

**INT. GUIDO'S KITCHEN — WALL TELEPHONE**

Guido is on the phone with his union boss.

> UNION BOSS: Nope. No deal. The strike's gonna go another month.

Guido hangs up, dismayed.

A knock at the door. Guido answers. His landlord is there.

> LANDLORD: Guido, you are a week late. If you don't give me a hundred bucks by tomorrow, I'll have you evicted.

Guido slams the door. His girlfriend comes out.

> GIRLFRIEND: You better go back to work. You were making good money before. I knew this strike was a bum idea. You need to get your job back.
>
> GUIDO: I can't. I'll be ostracized.
>
> GIRLFRIEND: Who cares what those lowlifes say? You have a family. What if I was pregnant? This whole you-not-working thing isn't working for me.
>
> GUIDO: I'm doing what I can.
>
> GIRLFRIEND: No, you're not. You need to get a hundred bucks by tomorrow, and you're sitting here talking to me. Get out there, and don't come back until you have a hundred.
>
> GUIDO: But what am I gonna do?
>
> GIRLFRIEND: You better think of something.

**Into the walk and carnival discovery.** The landlord's $100 demand and carnival's $100 prize are the same concrete objective. Don't introduce a different debt or extra amount before the first match.

Continuity: “What if I was pregnant?” is hypothetical, not confirmation of a pregnancy. “You have a family” does not establish children. Her “get your job back” line reflects her attitude: Guido remains a steelworker on strike, not a man already fired from the mill. The eviction threat is dialogue, not a statement of the lawful eviction timeline. Exact date and time of day remain unspecified.
