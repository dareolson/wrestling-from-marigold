Falls G3 r2: wiring and evidence (?falls=v1)

For Derek: G3 built the selector; this page is the wiring and the proof. Strikes are read at the hit (T3.5), tired throws land exceptional (T3.6), the back fall's arm step and the supported falls' phases are measured (T3.7, T3.8), a fall that does not fit the ring slides him inward (T3.9), the numbers from four uncapped AI-vs-AI runs (T3.10), and the snapmare re-filmed from the rear (T3.11). Decide: the knockdown rule given how rarely it fires, the back-fall arm slap, and whether the falls go standard. Not claimed: that any of it reads right. Speed:

Misses first

  • Strike knockdowns in real play: 1 in 601 game seconds of nothing-capped AI vs AI (4 runs). 35 strikes landed through _strikeReact, 1 of them left the receiver under 35 (the lowest stamina read right after a hit was 13.2). Stamina recovers in seconds and the AIs rarely wear each other down; this is the number, not retuned (the 35 line and the recovery are the game's). Derek's call: lower recovery, raise the line, or leave knockdowns as the rare worn-down event they are.
  • Back-fall arm step (T3.7): not a seam, left alone, and it is fast. At ?falls=v1 the near wrist moves george 52.4 px (shoulder 20.2, arm 30.2 deg); lou 84.2 px (shoulder 24.2, arm 41.3 deg) on the last frame into the mat (flag off: george 17.6 px; lou 19.8 px on its last fall frame), nearly all of it the arm swinging back down through ~40 deg in one frame (the free-fall timing puts the keys' arm swing in the last three frames); the frame after contact is 4.8 px. The last fall frame is the exact t = 1 pose, so nothing pops. If the slap reads too sudden, the cure is an earlier arm key, a Derek call.
  • Supported falls (T3.8): the keys already hold the catch, nothing changed. First support to the response start is forward-george 33 ms, forward-lou 33 ms, kneesForward-george 300 ms, kneesForward-lou 317 ms, sitBack-george 150 ms, sitBack-lou 200 ms, spill-george 150 ms, spill-lou 200 ms; 0 dead frames (no joint over 1 px/frame) anywhere in those windows. One stop-start, not a dead beat: kneesForward's fastest joint drops from 24-27 px/frame to 1.5 on the frame the knees take the weight, then eases back up over ten frames as the body folds down.
  • Ring edge (T3.9): inside the ropes at contact and through the landing in all 8 cases, but the sell outside in 2. The sell's writhe and clutch swing the limbs 13-18 px past the rope line in headbutt-lou-right, headbutt-lou-left; that is the sell's own motion (it plays on the mat whether or not the fall fits) and was not touched. The slide is the overshoot plus 10 px, over the fall; George lands 2-3 px inside the rope line at contact, Lou 7, so the margin is thin: the measured reach is per rig and the lying lengths are one table.
  • Checks of the 35 line (T3.5, real strikes): george jab: 40 -> 35.0 after the damage: read 35, stagger; george jab: 39.9 -> 34.9 after the damage: read 34.9, fall; lou jab: 40 -> 35.0 after the damage: read 35, stagger; lou jab: 39.9 -> 34.9 after the damage: read 34.9, fall.
  • Throws: the tired rule is wired to the slam, suplex and back body drop (receiver stamina read at the impact, before its damage; unit-tested at 24 / 25 / 26). In the four soak runs 3 big-throw landings, 0 of them exceptional (receiver under 25); bodySlam back [heavy] x2, backBodyDrop back [heavy] x1, piledriver limp [exceptional] x1. The tired-throw look is the filmed pair below (24 against 26 and fresh), staged. The piledriver, hip toss and snapmare keep their own tiers.
  • Snapmare (T3.11): re-filmed through the rear atomic-drop reversal (Arena._beginSnapmare), both rigs, both facings; the old G2 snapmare clips were face to face and are invalid.
