Bret Hart, WWF Royal Rumble SNES: "lockup / grapple stance" (INDEX rows 130–139, 233–236)

f130–f134: the grapple stance

  • Hands (2, 3): both forearms raised in front of the chest, elbows bent, hands near chin height and close together; they are carried, not reaching (no partner to hold).
  • Head (4): chin tucked, head forward of the shoulders; looks to the front, not to the side.
  • Stance (5): torso hunched forward, knees bent, hips back, feet close together and one foot slightly forward.
  • Near arm (D4): the camera-side (right of the sprite) arm is the one carried further forward; the other forearm is at the chest.
  • Not shown: arrival, collision, jockeying, drive, ropes, reversal (1, 2, 6–10).

f135–f139: the same stance turned side-on, then the arms dropping

  • Hands (3): f135–f136 one arm hangs low and slightly forward, the other is out of view behind the torso; f137–f139 the torso bends forward to about 45° with a forearm in front of the belly (a bend, not a reach).
  • Head (4): drops below the shoulder line from f137; the head is the lowest point of the upper body, a "go under" shape.
  • Stance (5, 7): weight on the back leg, knees bent, front foot flat; this is the closest frame set to a dominant "get lower" posture.
  • Not shown: contact with a partner.

Bret: the frames just before the suplex (INDEX 322–329, attacker)

  • What it shows: f322 a hunched, bent-kneed stand with both fists at the belly (the hold position); f323–f324 both arms go up, then the body stretches long (the lift); f325–f326 hunch forward with arms up and out.
  • Hands (3): they only exist as "holding something at belly height" and "above the head"; no neck or elbow hold is drawn.
  • Stance (5): f322 knees bent, hips back, feet about shoulder width apart, both feet flat.
  • Not shown: anything about the tie-up before the throw. I did not find a collar-and-elbow frame in this sheet.

Bret: the hip toss / arm drag (INDEX 345–356, the victim's sell, not the thrower)

  • What it shows: the man being pulled: back to the camera, one arm out to the side and trailing (f345–f347, an arm held out as if held by someone), then the torso twisting and tipping (f348–f350).
  • Near arm (D4, 7): the arm that trails is the one the partner would be holding; it is straight, the body leans away from it. That is the "defender hangs on the grabbed arm" shape, from the defender's side.
  • Not shown: the thrower and his grip.

Saturday Night Slam Masters (Mike Haggar): guard stance only

  • Stance (5): wide stance, knees bent, torso leaning a little forward, fists carried low at the sides to the belt; the head faces the front, chin level.
  • Hands (3): open fists at the belt, nothing near a neck or elbow.
  • No lockup row in frames 0–179 (checked); not usable for points 1–4, 6–10.

What the set gives us, and what it doesn't

  • Gives (by eye): hunched torso with knees bent and hips back (point 5); a "go lower" shape (f137–f139, point 7); the pulled man's arm trailing straight and the body leaning away from it (f345–f347).
  • Does not give: any hand on a neck or an elbow, heads over shoulders, arrival order, collision, drive, ropes, reversal. Those come from the plan's text and from the contact IK, not from these frames. Don't pose from these yet (T1.0).
